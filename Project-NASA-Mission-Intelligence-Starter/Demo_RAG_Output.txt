What emergency occurred during Apollo 13?

Retrieved Documents: 3

ANSWER:
During Apollo 13, an emergency occurred when an oxygen tank in the service module exploded. This incident led to a loss of oxygen and power, jeopardizing the mission and the safety of the crew. The crew had to use the lunar module as alifeboat to return to Earth. The mission ultimately required significant problem-solving and resourcefulness from both the astronauts and mission control to ensure a safe return.

What role did Mission Control play during Apollo 13?

Retrieved Documents: 3

ANSWER:
During Apollo 13, Mission Control played a critical role in managing the flight and ensuring the safety of the crew. The flight control team, led by Gene Krantz, was responsible for monitoring the spacecraft's systems and trajectory. They provided real-time communication and support to the astronauts, coordinating their actions and troubleshooting issues as they arose. For instance, Joe Kerwin served as the Capsule Communicator, maintaining direct communication with the crew. Mission Control was essential in executing the mission plan and responding to the emergency that occurred during the flight. 

(Source: Apollo 13, AS13_PAO_textract_full_text)

QUESTION 6
================================================================================
What communication systems were used during Apollo missions?

Retrieved Documents: 3

ANSWER:
During the Apollo missions, specifically Apollo 11, the communication systems included:

1. **S-band Communications System**: This system provided good-quality voice communication and was used for command module and lunar module up-data links. It also facilitated excellent real-time and playback telemetry performance.

2. **VHF (Very High Frequency) Link**: This was used for communication between the lunar module and the crew during extravehicular activities. The lunar module's VHF transmitter relayed crew voice and data to Earth.

3. **Television Transmission**: Color television pictures of high quality were transmitted from the command module, while good-quality black-and-white television pictures were received and converted during lunar surface operations.

These systems were generally consistent with those used in previous flights, as noted in the Apollo 11 mission documentation (NASA_NTRS_Archive_19710015566).
